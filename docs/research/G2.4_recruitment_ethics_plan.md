## Recruitment plan (per C2.5 §3, once Q4 clears)

### Target sample
- **Total: 30** (not 20-30 — C2.5 §2.3 is explicit that attrition at N=20 leaves too little to say anything; recruit to 30)
- **Split:** 15 Arm A (AfriMentor prototype) / 15 Arm B (generic LLM control)
- Block randomization within stratum, blocks of 4, allocation sequence pre-generated and held by someone not running that participant's session (C2.5 §3.3)

### Eligibility — from C2.5 §3.1/§3.2, not placeholder
**Include:**
- Age 18-35
- Currently operating a micro-business, or actively starting one within the last 12 months
- Informal/micro-enterprise sector: market trading, smallholder agribusiness, micro-manufacturing, small services
- Owns and personally uses an Android smartphone (iOS out of scope)
- Conversational in English or Nigerian Pidgin (pending Q2 — if prototype ends up English-only, this criterion tightens further)
- Passes the consent comprehension check (§4.4) — low literacy is not itself exclusionary, failure to understand participation is

**Exclude:**
- AfriMentor team members or immediate family
- Currently in a formal mentorship/financial-literacy programme (confounds pre/post)
- Participated in Sprint 1 intake research
- In acute financial distress where a 2-4 week study would delay real help (§4.6 — this needs a screening judgment call, and a stop rule if it comes up post-enrollment)

**Stratify on:** gender, self-reported baseline smartphone comfort (low/medium/high). Build both into the screening call — don't just capture them at consent.

### Recruitment channels — per C2.5 §3.3
Purposive recruitment through **market-trader associations and micro-enterprise cooperatives**, supplemented by **snowball referral**. Narrower than a general NGO/hub approach — you want groups whose members are already running an actual informal business, not just youth-entrepreneurship-adjacent programs.

Concrete targets once country is fixed:
- Local market-traders' associations (most Nigerian, Ghanaian, Kenyan, and Rwandan urban markets have a formal or informal traders' association leadership structure — Kigali's main markets included)
- Micro-enterprise/SME cooperatives and trade associations by sector (smallholder agribusiness cooperatives, artisan/manufacturing guilds) — in Rwanda this includes umbrella savings/cooperative structures tied to the national cooperative promotion body
- Existing project contacts from any prior fieldwork or Sprint 1 intake research (excluding those participants themselves, per E3) — their organizers are a good entry point even if the individuals aren't eligible

### Outreach approach
1. Identify 4-6 candidate associations/cooperatives in the chosen country
2. Approach through a named contact, ideally via a warm intro rather than cold outreach — trader associations are relationship-driven
3. Ask the association to help identify candidate members rather than doing an open call — keeps quality and trust high at N=30
4. Screen each candidate against eligibility (script below) before any consent conversation
5. Only proceed to the oral consent script (C2.5 §4) once eligible and stratum-assigned

### Screening call — aligned to actual eligibility
1. Confirm age (18-35) and that they're running or actively starting a micro-business
2. What does the business do? (free text — used to confirm informal/micro-enterprise sector fit, per C2.5 I2/I3)
3. Confirm personal Android smartphone ownership and use
4. Confirm English or Pidgin comfort
5. Ask (sensitively) whether they're currently in any formal mentorship or financial-literacy programme (E2)
6. Gender and self-reported smartphone comfort (low/medium/high) — for stratification
7. Availability for the 2-4 week window (pending Q6)
8. Flag, don't diagnose: if acute financial distress comes up unprompted, don't screen them out on the spot — note it and follow the §4.6 stop-rule process at consent instead, since that's a safeguarding decision, not a recruitment one
