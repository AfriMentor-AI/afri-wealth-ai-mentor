\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

In this paper, we introduced AfriMentor AI, an aligned language model mentorship system designed to support low-income African youth micro-entrepreneurs through personality-conditioned preference optimization and culturally grounded financial reasoning. Addressing the dual challenges of domain transfer and behavioral persona drift, our unified framework demonstrates that parameter-adapted fine-tuning (C2) and preference alignment (C3/C4) provide substantial advantages over baseline prompting (C1), yielding a $>113\%$ improvement in overall composite quality and elevating persona adherence from $0.200$ to $0.490+$ on held-out evaluations.

Crucially, our evaluation reinforces the empirical distinction between static self-reported traits and multi-turn behavioral consistency: incorporating conversational stability rewards directly into preference training measurably curtails persona drift over extended advisory dialogues. Coupled with a strict zero-tolerance safety guardrail ensuring $100\%$ block recall on high-risk financial advice, AfriMentor AI illustrates how specialized, character-aligned AI mentors can deliver contextually grounded, actionable, and safe entrepreneurial guidance at scale in the informal economy.

Beyond the technical contributions, our pilot deployment with $N=30$ informal-sector entrepreneurs across Nigeria, Ghana, and Kenya provides initial field evidence that personality-aligned AI mentorship produces meaningfully different engagement patterns and learning outcomes compared to a generic assistant baseline. Preliminary qualitative themes --- including voice as the natural interaction modality, contextual relevance as the primary engagement driver, and trust calibration as an ongoing design challenge --- offer grounded hypotheses for the full-scale evaluation. We distill these findings into seven design guidelines (DG1--DG7) organized across persona-alignment and deployment tiers, intended to support practitioners building similar AI mentorship systems for resource-constrained, informal-economy populations.

\paragraph{Future Work.} Three directions follow directly from this work. First, a full-scale randomized controlled trial ($N \ge 128$) is needed to test between-arm differences with adequate statistical power --- the pilot establishes feasibility and calibrates the required sample size, but cannot itself confirm persona superiority. Second, extending the corpus, voice pipeline, and evaluation suite to Swahili, Twi, and Yoruba is essential to reach the non-anglophone informal-sector populations that the current English/Pidgin restriction excludes. Third, longitudinal tracking beyond the 2--4 week pilot window is necessary to distinguish genuine behavioral change from novelty effects and to assess whether the anti-dependency design goal is achieved in practice --- whether users develop independent financial reasoning capabilities that persist after they stop using the system.
