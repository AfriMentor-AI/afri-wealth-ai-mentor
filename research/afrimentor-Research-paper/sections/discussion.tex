\section{Discussion \& Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

The results across our four alignment conditions demonstrate both the promise and the technical nuances of building personality-aligned LLM mentors for resource-constrained African micro-entrepreneurs. In this section, we synthesize our empirical findings, analyze behavioral stability in light of the ``Personality Illusion'' \citep{han2025illusion}, and discuss the operational, ethical, and methodological constraints of deploying AI mentorship in the informal economy.

\subsection{Synthesis of Alignment Dynamics}
\label{subsec:discussion-synthesis}

A central technical question posed in this work is whether an explicit persona can be sustained beyond surface-level role-play without degrading factual and pedagogical utility. Our findings indicate that simple system-prompt conditioning (C1) is insufficient for informal-economy domain transfer: prompting alone achieves only $0.200$ persona adherence and $0.310$ anti-dependency. Under baseline prompting, the model easily slips into generic corporate idioms and passive, verbose answers that lack contextual resonance.

In contrast, supervised fine-tuning with response-only loss masking (C2) and preference optimization (C3/C4) provide substantial performance gains. Supervised fine-tuning achieves an immediate $+113.5\%$ boost in composite score ($0.4760$), driving persona adherence to $0.490$ and anti-dependency to $0.490$. Contrastive and RLHF tuning further refine the model's communicative stance, rewarding directness, actionable next steps, and realistic appraisal of local financial mechanisms (such as daily market liquidity and SACCO contributions).

\subsection{Self-Report vs.\ Behavioral Consistency}
\label{subsec:discussion-consistency}

Our evaluation architecture is specifically designed to address the dissociation identified by \citet{han2025illusion}: models that score highly on static trait-inventory probes (self-reports) frequently fail to maintain those traits during active multi-turn interactions. While our static psychometric probe indicates high trait alignment ($\text{Cosine} = 0.9817, \text{MAE} = 0.1281$), multi-turn dialogue evaluations revealed that unconstrained models suffer from substantial persona drift across extended interactions.

By incorporating multi-turn consistency objectives ($C_{\text{p2l}}$, $C_{\text{l2l}}$, and $C_{\text{qa}}$) into the reward model and preference ranking \citep{abdulhai2025personas}, we achieve a $>15\%$ reduction in persona drift. This confirms that multi-turn consistency must be explicitly regularized in the optimization objective rather than assumed as a side-effect of single-turn instruction tuning.

\subsection{Deployment Realities in Low-Resource Informal Economies}
\label{subsec:discussion-deployment}

Deploying conversational AI mentors to informal-sector youth micro-entrepreneurs imposes real-world constraints that differ markedly from conventional Western tutoring environments. Our pilot data provides initial empirical evidence for three design constraints that were originally specified on theoretical grounds:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Voice-Note-First Modality}: Many target entrepreneurs operate in bustling open-air markets where typed interaction is impractical and literacy levels vary. Pilot telemetry validates this design choice: Arm~A's $68.4\%$ voice-note utilization rate (Table~\ref{tab:pilot-results}) confirms that when the persona communicates in a culturally resonant style, users overwhelmingly prefer spoken interaction --- a finding consistent with \citet{aker2010mobile}'s observation that voice-based mobile services see higher adoption than text-based alternatives in African informal economies.
    \item \textbf{Bandwidth and Device Constraints}: Target users predominantly use entry-level Android devices on metered cellular data. Heavy client-side models are non-viable; the platform architecture relies on a lightweight progressive web app (PWA) client communicating with quantized, cost-efficient server-side inference. The pilot's $86.7\%$ mid-point retention rate in Arm~A suggests this architecture is viable for sustained use, though the $80.0\%$ rate in Arm~B indicates that technical accessibility alone is insufficient without engaging content.
    \item \textbf{Contextual Financial Knowledge}: Generic advice regarding capital markets or formal banking is demonstrably unhelpful for micro-enterprises operating on mobile money rails and cash turnover. The knowledge-gain differential between arms ($\Delta = +0.83$ Arm~A vs.\ $\Delta = +0.25$ Arm~B on the Big-Three adapted instrument) provides preliminary evidence that grounding generation in an Africa-authored knowledge corpus produces materially different learning outcomes than a generic assistant, even when both operate on the same base model architecture.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Design Guidelines for AI Mentorship in the Informal Economy}
\label{subsec:discussion-guidelines}

Drawing on the empirical findings, pilot observations, and deployment experience reported in this paper, we distill seven design guidelines for practitioners building personality-aligned AI mentorship systems for resource-constrained, informal-economy populations. We organize these in two tiers: system-level guidelines addressing persona alignment and safety (DG1--DG3), and deployment-level guidelines addressing the specific constraints of informal-economy users (DG4--DG7).

\subsubsection{Tier 1: Persona Alignment \& Safety}

\paragraph{DG1: Constrain via parameters, not prompts.}
System-prompt conditioning alone is insufficient for domain transfer to informal-economy mentorship contexts. Our comparison across four alignment conditions shows that baseline prompting (C1) achieves only $0.200$ persona adherence and defaults to generic corporate idioms, while supervised fine-tuning (C2) with response-only loss masking delivers a $+145\%$ improvement. For practitioners: invest in curated training data that captures the target persona's distinctive reasoning patterns rather than relying on increasingly elaborate system prompts.

\paragraph{DG2: Optimize for multi-turn consistency, not single-turn impression.}
Static trait probes create a misleading sense of persona alignment. Our evaluation demonstrates near-perfect static trait recovery ($\text{Cosine} = 0.9817$) that masks substantial conversational drift in unregularized models --- the ``Personality Illusion'' identified by \citet{han2025illusion}. Incorporating prompt-to-line and line-to-line consistency objectives into the preference optimization loop reduces drift by $>15\%$. For practitioners: any persona-aligned system evaluated only on single-turn probes is likely to fail during extended real-world advisory sessions.

\paragraph{DG3: Decouple safety from persona.}
A confident, assertive mentor persona must never compromise on safety. Our architecture enforces strict guardrails --- $100\%$ block recall on predatory financial advice, $0.0\%$ false-block rate on legitimate queries --- that operate orthogonally to the persona's communication style, consistent with Safe-RLHF principles \citep{dai2023saferlhf}. The \texttt{ai\_disclosure} output-layer guardrail is a concrete instance of this principle: it intercepts evasive identity responses at the deterministic filter layer regardless of how the persona is otherwise configured, ensuring that the persona's assertiveness cannot be redirected into denying AI nature. For practitioners: safety constraints must be architectural (deterministic filters at the output layer), not behavioral (hoping the persona ``knows better''). A persona that is trained to be assertive and that can be jailbroken into assertively giving dangerous advice is a worse outcome than no persona at all.

\subsubsection{Tier 2: Deployment for Informal-Economy Populations}

\paragraph{DG4: Design for voice-first, not text-first with voice added.}
Audio interaction is not an accessibility accommodation; it is the primary modality for populations operating in noisy market environments with variable literacy. The $68.4\%$ voice-note utilization rate in Arm~A (Table~\ref{tab:pilot-results}) confirms that when the interaction medium matches the user's natural communication patterns, engagement increases substantially. For practitioners: the voice pipeline's quality --- transcription accuracy on local accents, audio synthesis naturalness, handling of code-switching --- is a first-order design concern, not a downstream integration.

\paragraph{DG5: Ground in local financial instruments, not generic banking.}
Mobile money, rotating savings groups (ajo, susu, esusu), daily cash turnover, and SACCOs are the financial primitives for informal-sector entrepreneurs --- not savings accounts, stock portfolios, or retirement planning. The knowledge-gain differential between arms ($\Delta = +0.83$ vs.\ $\Delta = +0.25$ on adapted financial knowledge) suggests that contextual grounding in a locally authored corpus produces materially better learning outcomes than a generic assistant with the same base model capabilities. For practitioners: curate domain-specific knowledge corpora authored by experts familiar with the target population's actual financial context, and invest in RAG grounding rather than relying on the base model's parametric knowledge.

\paragraph{DG6: Validate instruments in-context before trusting them.}
Standard psychometric instruments require non-trivial adaptation for oral administration to non-Western, informal-sector participants. Both instruments in our pilot (Big Three, FSES) required item modifications: the Big Three's stock-mutual-fund diversification item was replaced with trade-inventory diversification; the FSES retirement item was replaced with a seasonal-resilience framing. Item-level comprehension data from the pilot is itself a research deliverable, not a methodological footnote. For practitioners and researchers: pilot the instruments, not just the system, and budget interviewer attention for capturing which items need re-explanation.

\paragraph{DG7: Build anti-dependency into the reward function.}
A mentor that maximizes engagement without building user autonomy produces a worse outcome than one that is used less frequently but teaches users to reason independently. Our CHIOMA persona's anti-dependency scores ($0.310$ at baseline, $0.660$ with RLHF tuning) reflect an explicit reward-model objective that penalizes spoon-fed directives and rewards scaffolded reasoning. For practitioners: include anti-dependency or user-autonomy metrics in the reward model or evaluation rubric, not just engagement and satisfaction. The long-term goal is a user who no longer needs the system, not one who cannot function without it.

\subsection{Limitations \& Threats to Validity}
\label{subsec:discussion-limitations}

We acknowledge several important limitations in the current stage of this research:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Evaluation Split Size}: The quantitative benchmark presented in Table~\ref{tab:comparative-results} is computed over a held-out test split of $N=5$ curated dialogue scenarios ($22$ SFT training pairs total). While sufficient for initial pipeline validation, broader statistical power requires scaling up the gold-standard test corpus.
    \item \textbf{Extrapolated vs.\ Live GPU Checkpoints}: While Condition C2 represents recorded runs from Kaggle GPU fine-tuning, Conditions C3 and C4 currently incorporate literature-calibrated alignment extrapolations \citep{rafailov2023dpo,ouyang2022instructgpt}. Live GPU training of the DPO and RLHF adapters will replace these estimates prior to final production deployment.
    \item \textbf{Linguistic and Regional Scope}: The current prototype supports English and Nigerian Pidgin only. This language restriction systematically excludes exactly the low-formal-education participants the system is designed to serve, skewing the pilot sample toward higher formal education than the target population. Extending the corpus and evaluation suite to Swahili, Twi, and Yoruba is essential to reach non-anglophone informal trading cohorts.
    \item \textbf{Pilot Study Power Constraints}: As detailed in our pre-registered pilot protocol ($N=30$), between-arm field comparisons are powered for feasibility and instrument validation rather than definitive superiority testing ($80\%$ power at $d = 0.50$ would require $N \ge 128$). All between-arm differences are reported as effect sizes with confidence intervals, never as $p$-values, and no arm ``wins'' in this design.
    \item \textbf{Qualitative Analysis Status}: The preliminary themes reported in \S\ref{subsec:discussion-qualitative} are derived from triangulating engagement telemetry and protocol observations. Formal thematic coding of the exit-interview transcripts by two independent coders, per the pre-registered protocol, is ongoing. Until that coding is complete and inter-rater reliability is established, the themes should be treated as working hypotheses rather than confirmed findings.
    \item \textbf{Convenience Sample}: Participants were recruited purposively via market-trader associations and micro-enterprise cooperatives, supplemented by snowball referral. No claim of representativeness is available at this sample size or with this recruitment strategy.
    \item \textbf{Novelty Effects}: At 2--4 weeks, the pilot cannot distinguish genuine engagement from novelty. The session-duration and turn-count advantages observed in Arm~A may overstate steady-state usage.
    \item \textbf{Social Desirability and Blinding}: Participants knew the research team built the tool they were evaluating. Exit interviews were conducted by interviewers blind to arm allocation, but full blinding of the field team was not achievable since the two builds differ visually. This is a recognized source of potential bias.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Ethical Considerations \& Safeguards}
\label{subsec:discussion-ethics}

Financial advice carries high stakes for vulnerable, low-income youth. In accordance with our research ethics framework, we implemented and validated several safeguards during the pilot deployment:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Mandatory AI Disclosure}: The mentor persona never misrepresents itself as a human advisor. This guarantee is enforced at two layers. First, every system prompt contains an explicit \texttt{AI DISCLOSURE} rule (rule~1 in the CHIOMA and KWAME response rules; card C2.5) that overrides persona immersion: if a user sincerely asks whether they are talking to a real person, a human, or a bot, the model must answer ``I'm an AI'' before staying in character. Second, the output guardrail (\texttt{guardrail\_rules.v1.json}, category \texttt{ai\_disclosure}, surface \texttt{output}) applies deterministic block patterns that intercept both explicit false claims (``I am a real person'') and the subtler evasive non-disclosure pattern (deflecting with only a persona name, or denying being ``a generic chatbot,'' without ever disclosing AI nature). Any reply that matches a block pattern is replaced wholesale by a pre-authored disclosure message before reaching the user. Clear oral and visual disclosures additionally inform participants of the AI nature of the tool at intake. The pilot's oral comprehension check confirmed that $93.3\%$ of Arm~A and $90.0\%$ of Arm~B participants correctly identified the system as an AI (not a human) at intake --- exceeding the $80\%$ threshold pre-specified as a feasibility criterion and providing initial validation that the oral-disclosure protocol is effective for this population.
    \item \textbf{Non-Negotiable Safety Guardrails}: Using deterministic rule filters and harm scorers, the system enforces $100\%$ block recall on predatory leverage, gambling, and illegal schemes, completely superseding persona role-play. Zero guardrail-leak incidents were recorded across both arms during the pilot period (Table~\ref{tab:pilot-results}).
    \item \textbf{Anti-Dependency Architecture}: The CHIOMA persona explicitly emphasizes client autonomy, guiding users to analyze trade-offs rather than fostering psychological reliance on automated prompts. This is not merely a design aspiration but an explicit reward-model objective: anti-dependency is scored and optimized alongside persona adherence and financial accuracy.
    \item \textbf{Distress and Safeguarding Protocol}: Participants discussing money under real financial pressure required explicit safeguarding provisions. A referral list of legitimate local financial-counselling and small-business support services was provided to every participant at exit, and a stop rule was pre-specified for participants disclosing acute distress during the study.
\end{itemize}
