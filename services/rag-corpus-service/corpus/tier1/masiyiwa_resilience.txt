Strive Masiyiwa: Resilience, Faith, and Fighting for African Telecoms

Strive Masiyiwa is the founder of Econet Wireless, one of Africa's most successful telecommunications companies. Born in Zimbabwe in 1961, he is one of the continent's most prominent business leaders and philanthropists.

On the five-year legal battle:
When Masiyiwa sought a mobile phone licence in Zimbabwe in 1993, the government refused. The state-owned telecoms company had a monopoly and the government moved to block any competition. Masiyiwa took the government to court — a decision that led to five years of legal battles, death threats, and financial ruin at points. Many people told him to give up. He did not. In 1998, the Supreme Court of Zimbabwe ruled in his favour. Econet launched and became one of the most successful telecoms businesses in Africa.

On what sustained him:
Masiyiwa has spoken publicly and extensively about the role of faith in sustaining him through the five years. He describes reading Psalm 46 repeatedly during the hardest periods. He is not prescriptive about religion for others, but he is clear that having a foundation of belief — something larger than the business itself — was what stopped him from surrendering when the pressure was at its greatest.

On the African entrepreneur's unique challenge:
Masiyiwa argues that African entrepreneurs face obstacles that entrepreneurs in developed markets simply do not encounter — unstable currencies, unreliable infrastructure, political interference in business, and legal systems that can be weaponised against challengers to established power. His response is not to complain but to build differently. He built Econet to operate in difficult environments by design — robust systems, local partnerships, and a long time horizon.

On mentorship:
Masiyiwa is one of Africa's most prominent advocates for mentorship. Through his Facebook page, which has millions of followers, he shares business lessons drawn from his experience. He believes strongly that knowledge must flow from those who have succeeded to those who are starting. He describes receiving mentorship from older businesspeople in his early career as transformative.

On giving back:
The Higherlife Foundation, founded by Masiyiwa and his wife Tsitsi, has supported the education of over 250,000 orphaned and vulnerable children in Zimbabwe, Zambia, Lesotho, and beyond. Masiyiwa argues that business success in Africa carries a community obligation — that wealth generated on the continent must be reinvested in the continent's people.

Key lessons for African entrepreneurs:
1. Persistence through systemic obstruction is the defining test of the African entrepreneur
2. A foundation of belief sustains you when logic says stop
3. Build your business to operate in difficult environments — do not wait for conditions to improve
4. Seek mentors actively and share knowledge generously when you succeed
5. Business success and community obligation are not separate — they are one
