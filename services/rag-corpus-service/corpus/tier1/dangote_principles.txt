Aliko Dangote: Business Principles and the African Market Opportunity

Aliko Dangote is the founder and chairman of Dangote Group, one of Africa's largest conglomerates. Born in Kano, Nigeria in 1957, he started his business career with a loan of 500,000 naira from his uncle at the age of 21.

On starting from nothing:
Dangote began by trading commodities — cement, sugar, flour, and salt. He understood that Nigerians needed basic goods every day, and he focused on making those goods available reliably and affordably. He reinvested every naira of profit back into the business in the early years, never spending on luxury until the business foundation was solid.

On the African market:
Dangote has consistently argued that Nigeria and Africa represent one of the greatest untapped business opportunities in the world. He famously said that Nigeria is one of the best-kept secrets. While others looked abroad for opportunity, he looked inward. He saw that Nigeria was importing products it could manufacture locally — cement, sugar, flour, pasta — and systematically moved into each sector.

On persistence through difficulty:
Building a business in Nigeria meant navigating currency devaluations, infrastructure failures, inconsistent power supply, and bureaucratic obstacles. Dangote's response was never to relocate or give up, but to build his own infrastructure — his own power plants, his own logistics fleet, his own port. He turned each obstacle into a vertical integration opportunity.

On hiring and talent:
Dangote repeatedly emphasized the importance of hiring the best people and paying them well. He believed that surrounding yourself with excellent people was the single most important multiplier for business growth. He invested in training and retaining talent even when it was expensive.

On reinvestment:
The Dangote philosophy is simple — take the profit from one business and reinvest it into the next. Sugar trading funded cement trading. Cement trading funded cement manufacturing. Manufacturing funded petrochemicals. Each business funded the next, building a diversified empire step by step over four decades.

Key lessons for African entrepreneurs:
1. Start with what people need every day — food, shelter, basic materials
2. Find what your country imports and ask why it cannot be made locally
3. Reinvest profits relentlessly in the early years
4. Build your own infrastructure when the environment fails you
5. Hire well and pay well — talent is your most important asset
6. Think in decades, not quarters
